\begin{abstract}
Steganography conceals secrets by embedding them in innocuous covers so that their presence is not obvious. Linguistic steganography hides data within natural-language text, but unlike image covers, text offers little usable redundancy-making capacity, fluency, and resistance to steganalysis harder to reconcile than in classical image hiding. Large language models (LLMs) have partly closed this gap by enabling fluent generation, rewriting, retrieval, and prompt-driven protocols that extend beyond hand-crafted lexical substitution. However, published work at this intersection has grown rapidly and uses heterogeneous terms, baselines, and metrics, motivating a transparent systematic synthesis. We conducted a systematic literature review (2018-2025) following established SLR practice, searching five major bibliographic databases to identify 1043 candidate records; after deduplication, screening, and full-text assessment, 26 primary studies met our inclusion criteria. These were synthesized across approaches, application domains, evaluation methods, external knowledge use, and technical trade-offs. The evidence suggests that while LLM-based methods often improve contextual plausibility relative to earlier linguistic techniques, payload, statistical naturalness, and security remain in tension-leaving open challenges in standardized evaluation, robustness across tokenization and channels, and practical deployment.
\end{abstract}
