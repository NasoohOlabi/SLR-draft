\begin{abstract}
	his systematic literature review examines how Large Language Models (LLMs) are reshaping linguistic steganography. Through
	analysis of primary studies, the review synthesizes recent work on generative, rewriting-based, black-box, and context-aware
	approaches, with particular attention to application domains, evaluation practices, external knowledge integration, and recurring
	technical trade-offs. The surveyed literature suggests that LLM-based methods can improve text fluency and contextual plausibility
	relative to earlier linguistic steganography techniques, but it also shows that gains in payload, naturalness, and security remain
	difficult to balance in practice. A recurring theme across the reviewed studies is the growing importance of contextual compatibility,
	including domain adaptation, retrieval, and semantic guidance, as a mechanism for improving both usability and concealment. The
	review concludes by identifying unresolved challenges in evaluation standardization, robustness, tokenization reliability, and practical
	deployment.

\end{abstract}
