Linguistic steganography hides secrets inside ordinary sentences, but natural language offers limited redundancy for concealment \cite{yang2020vae,kaptchuk2021meteor}. A single awkward synonym, statistically unusual phrase, or contextually implausible sentence can expose hidden communication. Classical approaches based on lexical or syntactic modification therefore tend to provide low payloads and leave detectable artifacts that modern steganalysis can exploit \cite{Wang_2023}.

Large language models have changed this landscape by making high-quality text generation widely accessible. Their fluency, contextual sensitivity, and broad domain coverage have enabled a new generation of steganographic methods that rely on text generation, paraphrasing, retrieval, and prompting rather than only local word substitution.

These advances do not eliminate the core trade-offs of the field. Increasing embedding capacity can still degrade fluency, coherence, or statistical naturalness, while optimizing for stealth may reduce throughput or robustness. This tension is often described as the Perceptual-Statistical Imperceptibility Conflict (PSIC) \cite{yang2020vae}. Against this background, a systematic synthesis is needed to clarify how recent work defines the problem, evaluates success, and balances practical constraints.

This review addresses that need by identifying and synthesizing recent primary studies on LLM-based linguistic steganography. Rather than arguing that a single method has resolved the field's long-standing challenges, the paper maps the emerging design space, highlights recurring evaluation practices, and identifies the main limitations that remain unresolved.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 introduces the background on steganography, its evaluation criteria and the capabilities of LLMs and their role in generative linguistic steganography. Section 3 reviews related surveys to position the present study. Section 4 outlines the review methodology, including the research questions, search strategy, and selection criteria. Section 5 presents the findings across five research questions (RQ1--RQ5). Section 6 discusses the implications and limitations of the review. Section 7 concludes the paper and outlines future research directions.
