\section{Conclusion}

The 32 studies reviewed here confirm that LLMs have meaningfully expanded the design space of linguistic steganography. Methods once confined to local word substitution now encompass full generative pipelines, rewriting systems, black-box prompting frameworks, and context-aware retrieval architectures---all pursuing some combination of improved fluency, contextual plausibility, and usable payload.

What has not changed is the fundamental difficulty. Evaluation practices remain inconsistent across studies, and the persistent tensions among imperceptibility, capacity, robustness, and deployability are not resolved. The PSIC effect, low-entropy communication settings, tokenization ambiguity, and the practical constraints of black-box access all continue to limit what current systems can reliably achieve. Reported improvements frequently depend on different baselines, datasets, and evaluation protocols, which makes cross-paper comparison genuinely difficult.

What emerged most clearly across the reviewed literature is that contextual compatibility---the degree to which stego text fits its intended communicative setting, not just its local fluency---is becoming a practical necessity rather than a secondary concern. Domain adaptation, retrieval, semantic guidance, entity and emotion control, stylistic constraints, and token-level quality control are all, at bottom, different approaches to the same problem: making generated text plausible as a communicative act in context. Future progress in the field will depend on stronger evaluation protocols and more disciplined integration of context, not only on more capable base models.

The most pressing gaps are well-defined: standardized evaluation with shared benchmarks, reliable extraction under realistic tokenization and editing conditions, robustness-aware capacity reporting, stronger security guarantees for deployed systems, and clearer analytical boundaries between linguistic steganography and adjacent problems such as watermarking and AI provenance. On these counts, the literature shows a field that is technically active but not yet methodologically mature.
